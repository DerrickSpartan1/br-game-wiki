| --- | --- |

## Real-world notes

500 mm barrel; about 740 m/s; one of the first straight-line stock designs, and with the side magazine it keeps the stock close to the bore line to help control bursts; flip-up front post and folding rear aperture sight marked 100–1,200 m; dovetail scope mount on the receiver for the ZFG 42 or ZF4 scope; the charging lever doubles as the safety; folding bipod; flip-out spike bayonet under the barrel; its muzzle brake cut recoil and flash but made a loud blast; in real life the side mag and full-power round made it feel unbalanced, and bursts were hard to control; firing auto from an open bolt kept the chamber open between bursts to stop rounds cooking off (flavor only, since the game has no overheating); after the war its layout and gas-rotating bolt fed into the US M60 and the Swiss Stgw 52

## In-game traits

One roster entry that rolls one of 3 versions (see [Versions](#versions) below). Its open-bolt delay only applies on auto; semi-auto fires from a closed bolt with no delay (see [Rules](../../rules.md#open-bolt-delay)).

## Attachments

AR/LMG/battle rifle family attachments; native dovetail scope mount. Unique: the ZFG 42 or ZF4 scope, the folding bipod, the flip-out spike bayonet, and the Schießbecher grenade cup with its own grenade sight (replaces the muzzle brake). Mag tiers (best guess, review later): 10 standard; 20-round as makeshift extended and quick; higher tiers TBD

## Versions

3 versions after merging. The Germans called every model just the FG 42; collectors' "Model I" and "Model II" names are the Type E and Type G. Prototype Types A to D merge into the Type E, and the first stamped-receiver Type F into the Type G. Modern semi-auto replicas, like Sport-Systeme Dittrich's BD 42, weren't on the sources checked (see the [open questions](../../planning/open-questions.md)).
