# SVT-40

| Spec | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Category | Battle rifle |
| Cartridge | Varies by version: 7.62×54mmR (main), 7.62×39mm, or 7.5×55mm Swiss |
| History | USSR; designed by Fedor Tokarev for a semi-auto replacement of the Mosin-Nagant, partly inspired by the [Mondragón rifle](mondragon.md), beating Sergei Simonov's AVS-36; adopted in 1938 as the SVT-38 (about 150,000 made until April 1940); after Winter War complaints it became the lighter SVT-40, made from July 1940 at Tula and then other arsenals; about 70,000 made in 1940, over a million in 1941, and 264,000 in 1942; production ended in January 1945; about 1.5–1.6 million SVT-38s and SVT-40s in all (sources differ); the Mosin-Nagant stayed the standard rifle, and the cheaper [PPSh-41](../smgs/ppsh-41.md) drew production away; Finland captured thousands and kept them until 1958, and Germany used several hundred thousand captured ones as the SIG 258(r) and 259(r); out of Soviet service by 1955 |

## Real-world notes

3.85 kg; 1,226 mm long; 610–625 mm barrel (sources differ); about 830–840 m/s; the gas piston sits above the barrel, and its tilting bolt is the same system later used in the FN FAL; muzzle brake (six vents on the SVT-38, cut to two per side later); folding magazine release; bayonet; in real life its birch stocks cracked at the wrist from recoil, shots strayed up and down, early mags fell out, and poorly trained troops struggled to maintain it (flavor only); Germany valued it because it took a bayonet, unlike their Gewehr 43, and studied its gas system while developing that rifle; about 7,500 retired Finnish rifles were sold to US collectors

## In-game traits

One roster entry that rolls one of 6 versions (see [Versions](#versions) below). The AVT-40 version keeps its real auto mode, which was known as hard to control.

## Attachments

AR/LMG/battle rifle family attachments; adapter scope mount, except the sniper version, which spawns with its PU scope. Unique: the bayonet and the PU 3.5× scope. Mag tiers (best guess, review later): 10 standard; experimental 15-round as makeshift extended and quick; experimental drum as extended (capacity TBD); extended quick TBD
