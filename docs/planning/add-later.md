# Guns to add later

Guns and offshoots found while researching, waiting for their turn. Offshoots name the category they'd likely go in. Battle rifles and DMRs are now one category, so offshoots marked for either go there.

- **Tokarev TT-33:** its cartridge was derived from the C96's 7.63mm, so they could share ammo.
- **Stechkin APS:** Soviet machine pistol with a holster-stock.
- **Welrod:** WWII British bolt-action pistol with a built-in suppressor.
- **Lancaster pistol:** four-barrel pistol.
- **Gyrojet:** fired small rockets instead of bullets.
- **Nagant M1895:** classic revolver.
- **Webley:** classic British revolver.
- **Colt Single Action Army:** classic revolver.
- **Janz-Schuknecht Pistole:** Janz's 9mm semi-auto.
- **FN Five-seven:** the P90's companion pistol, also in 5.7×28mm.
- **Ruger-57:** 5.7×28mm pistol, offered since 2019.
- **Kel-Tec P50:** 5.7×28mm pistol (2021) that takes the P90's 50-round mags.
- **PSA Rock 5.7:** Palmetto State Armory's 5.7×28mm pistol (2022).
- **S&W M&P 5.7:** Smith & Wesson's 5.7×28mm pistol (2023).
- **AR-57:** AR-platform 5.7×28mm rifle fed from 50-round P90 mags.
- **CMMG Banshee (5.7):** AR-style guns offered in 5.7×28mm.
- **ST Kinetics CPW:** multi-caliber SMG/PDW that can be set up for 5.7×28mm by swapping the barrel and mag.
- **Excel Arms 5.7 guns:** four 5.7×28mm firearms; models to research.
- **MasterPiece Arms 5.7 guns:** three 5.7×28mm firearms; models to research.
- **HK UCP:** the MP7's cancelled companion pistol in 4.6×30mm; prototype only.
- **PSA X5.7 PDW:** MP7-style concept gun in 5.7×28mm shown in 2024.
- **KRISS KARD:** semi-auto pistol announced in 2010 using a smaller Super V system, in 9×19 and .45 ACP; status to research.
- **PP-19-01 Vityaz:** the Bizon's 9×19 successor with box mags; Russia's standard SMG since 2005.
- **PPK-20:** subcompact modernization of the Vityaz.
- **StG 45(M):** late-war roller-delayed prototype in 7.92×33mm Kurz; its action led to HK's G3 and MP5.
- **Volkssturmgewehr 1-5:** last-ditch gas-delayed rifle in 7.92×33mm Kurz.
- **MKb 42(W):** Walther's competing design to the StG 44's predecessor.
- **Ingram SAM rifles:** M1 carbine derivatives in 5.56×45mm (M16 mags), 7.62×39mm (AK mags), and 7.62×51mm (FAL mags).
- **Hezi SM-1:** Israeli bullpup conversion of the M1 carbine.
- **vz. 58 offshoots:** EZ-B bullpup prototype, KLEČ light machine gun (longer barrel, like the RPK), and vz. 58/97 marksman rifle (possible DMR).
- **AR-18 offshoots:** AR-18 LSW prototype (quick-change barrel, later converted to 7.62×39mm; possible LMG), Singapore's SAR-80, SAR-87, and SR-88, and the Australian Bushmaster M17S bullpup. The Howa Type 89 is already on the possible-additions list.
- **Stoner 63 LMG configurations (LMG category):** belt-fed LMG (open bolt, 75-, 100-, or 150-round belt containers, quick-change barrel), tripod MMG, vehicle fixed machine gun, and the SEALs' Commando / Mk 23 Mod 0 (100-round box, no quick-change barrel).
- **Stoner 63 descendants:** Knight's Armament Stoner LMG (based on the later Stoner 86) and the semi-auto Robinson Armament M96 Expeditionary Rifle.
- **M16 / M4 offshoots:** Colt 9mm SMG (SMG category), Mk 12 SPR and M16A1 Special High and Low Profile (DMR category), Philippine Marine Scout Sniper Rifle, Taiwan's T65, T86, and T91, and Iran's KH-2002 bullpup.
- **HK33 offshoots:** HK33SG/1 marksman rifle (DMR category), and the HK13 light machine gun (box or 100-round drum, quick-change barrel), belt-fed HK23, and Myanmar MA-12 (LMG category).
- **Galil offshoots:** Galatz / Galil Sniper (semi-auto 7.62×51mm with a heavy match barrel and two-stage trigger), its SR-99 update and Vietnamese SBT-7.62VN clone (sniper or DMR category), and the 5.56mm Marksman Assault Rifle Mk 1 (DMR category).
- **AK-74 offshoots:** RPK-74 and RPK-16 (LMG category), and the AM-17 compact (see its own entry below).
- **AUG offshoots:** AUG HBAR and HBAR-T (24.4 in heavy barrel, bipod, fires from an open bolt; LMG category), and the AICW (experimental F88 with a Metal Storm 30mm barrel on top; possible special or crate weapon).
- **FAMAS offshoots:** FA-MAS Type 62 (7.62×51mm prototype from the 1950s–60s; battle rifle category) and the HS Produkt VHS (Croatian bullpup).
- **SA80 offshoots:** L86 LSW (25.4 in heavy barrel and bipod; LMG or DMR category), EM-2 (1950s British .280 bullpup, briefly adopted as the Rifle No. 9), and the EIW97 (1990s SA80 testbed firing electrically primed ammo, with programmable bursts at 300–400 RPM).
- **AS Val offshoots:** VSS Vintorez (suppressed 9×39mm sniper rifle, usually semi-auto with 10-round mags but capable of full auto), VSSM (modernized), and KO VSS (civilian semi-auto); DMR or sniper category.
- **SG 550 offshoots:** SG 550-1 Sniper (25.6 in heavy barrel; DMR or sniper category), SG 750 (6.5mm Creedmoor) and SG 751 SAPR (7.62×51mm, the Swiss Army's DMR; battle rifle or DMR category), the SG 540/542/543 predecessors, and the SG 510 / Stgw 57 (7.5×55mm Swiss battle rifle).
- **QBZ-95 offshoots:** QJB-95, QJB-95-1, and QJB-97 squad machine guns (bipod, heavier barrel, 75- or 80-round drums; LMG category), the K12 PUMA (12-gauge bullpup shotgun styled after the QBZ-95; shotgun category), and the QBZ-191 (China's newer conventional-layout rifle; possible AR addition).
- **G36 offshoots:** MG36 (heavy barrel squad machine gun with the C-Mag; LMG category), SL8 (semi-auto sporting rifle with a thumbhole stock, 20.1 in heavy barrel, and 10-round mags; possible DMR), the HK XM8 (US prototype rifle based on the G36), and the Mexican FX-05 Xiuhcoatl.
- **Tavor offshoots:** Tavor 7 (7.62×51mm bullpup with 17 in or 20 in barrel; battle rifle category).
- **SCAR offshoots:** SCAR-H / Mk 17 and the civilian SCAR 17S (7.62×51mm, 20-round mags, 550 RPM, CQC, STD, and LB barrels; battle rifle category), Mk 17 Mod 1 (6.5mm Creedmoor), SCAR-H PR, Mk 20 SSR, and SCAR 20S in 7.62×51mm and 6.5mm Creedmoor (semi-auto precision rifles; DMR or sniper category), HAMR IAR (switched from closed bolt to open bolt as the barrel heated; lost the Marine IAR contest to the [HK416](../weapons/assault-rifles/hk416.md)-based M27; LMG category), and HAMR NGSW (6.8mm entry in the Army's Next Generation Squad Weapon program).
- **HK416 offshoots:** M38 SDMR (the M27 with a Leupold 2.5–8×36 scope and QDSS suppressor, select-fire, used to 600 m) and Norway's HK416N S sharpshooter (DMR category), the HK417 (7.62×51mm; battle rifle category), and the HK433 (H&K's newer modular rifle; possible assault rifle addition).
- **SIG MCX offshoots:** MCX-SPEAR / M7 (scaled-up MCX in 6.8×51mm Common Cartridge, the US Army's NGSW rifle since 2022; battle rifle category), MCX-MR (7.62×51mm, 16 in fluted barrel, 20-round SR-25 mags, select-fire; failed Army CSASS entry; DMR or battle rifle category), and the SIG MPX (9×19 submachine gun whose piston system the MCX grew from; SMG category).
- **AK-12 offshoots:** AK-308 (7.62×51mm, 20-round mags, based on the AK-15) and AKV-721 (civilian two-receiver 7.62×51mm design) for the battle rifle category, the RPL-20 (belt-fed 5.45mm prototype that fires from an open bolt; LMG category), the AKV-521 (civilian semi-auto two-receiver design; caliber to look up), and the AK-22 (2023 prototype in 6.02×41mm, Russia's answer to the US NGSW; possible addition).
- **AM-17:** Kalashnikov's AKS-74U replacement, a separate design from the AK-12, based on Dragunov's MA: short-stroke piston, hinged two-part polymer receiver, 230 mm barrel, 2.5 kg, about 850 RPM, 30-round 5.45mm mags; first batch September 2025. Its AMB-17 is integrally suppressed in 9×39mm. Possible assault rifle addition.
- **Mondragón M1893 and M1894:** Manuel Mondragón's straight-pull bolt-action rifles, built by SIG (50 and then 200 made), in 6.5×48mm or 5.2×68mm Rubin, fed from fixed 8-round en-bloc clips; a rapid setting fires the shot as the bolt closes (sniper or special category).
- **M1 Garand offshoots:** T25 / T47 .30 Light Rifle (Earle Harvey's 7.62×49mm design that lost to the T44, which became the [M14](../weapons/battle-rifles/m14.md); a separate design, dropped in 1953; possible battle rifle) and the T31 bullpup prototype (possible battle rifle).
- **SVT-40 offshoots:** Simonov's AVS-36 (1936 select-fire 7.62×54mmR rifle, used in the Winter War and withdrawn in 1941; possible battle rifle) and Tokarev's AT-44 (scaled-down 7.62×41mm assault rifle prototype with a bipod and pistol grip; assault rifle category).
- **FG 42 offshoots:** the US M60 (LMG category) and the Swiss Stgw 52 prototype, both of which drew on its design. Walther's G 41(W), the Army rifle offered in its place, is now a version on the [Gewehr 43](../weapons/battle-rifles/gewehr-43.md) page.
- **Gewehr 43 offshoots:** Mauser's G 41(M) (the only design that met the 1940 rules: striker-fired, rotating bolt, muzzle-trap gas system; 6,673 made, nearly 1,700 sent back as unusable; possible battle rifle) and Mauser's Gerät 03 (pressed-steel roller-locked prototype that passed a 5,000-round trial and led to roller-delayed blowback; ties to the StG 45(M) above).
- **SKS offshoots:** the Chinese Type 63 (early-1960s assault rifle meant to replace the Type 56 carbine; failed PLA standards; assault rifle category) and Simonov's PTRS-41 anti-tank rifle, whose mechanism the SKS borrowed (special category).
- **MAS-49 offshoots:** the MAS-54, a 7.62×51mm rifle meant to replace the MAS-49 that never succeeded (possible battle rifle; the FA-MAS Type 62 under the FAMAS offshoots was the other attempt).
- **FN FAL offshoots:** the heavy-barrel squad versions with bipods and 30-round mags (FAL 50.42 / FALO, Canadian C2 and C2A1, Australian L2A1, South African R1 HB, and Israel's Makle'a; LMG category), the 5.56mm FN CAL (an unsuccessful FAL-like assault rifle; assault rifle category), and Brazil's IMBEL IA2 and MD97 (assault rifle category).
- **AR-10 offshoots:** belt-fed AR-10 LMG prototypes (feed problems never solved; LMG category), and other makers' 7.62mm AR-10-pattern rifles, each its own gun (battle rifle & DMR category): IWI Zion-25, LMT LM308MWS, Colt CM901 / LE901, SIG Sauer 716i, Smith & Wesson M&P10, Ruger SR-762, Sako M23, and BCL 102. The SR-25 / M110 and HK417 are already queued.
- **SG 510 predecessors:** the Swiss SK 46 (gas-operated) and AK 53 (blow-forward: the barrel moves forward instead of the bolt moving back); possible battle rifles, and the AK 53 could be a funny gun.
- **CETME offshoots:** the CETME Model L, a downsized 5.56×45mm rifle that was Spain's service rifle from 1984 until the G36 replaced it in 1999 (assault rifle category).
- **HK G3 offshoots:** the HK21 and HK11 light machine guns built on the G3 system, and Myanmar's BA64 (LMG category), the PSG1 (semi-auto precision rifle with a 650 mm barrel; sniper category), and France's Carabine Mitrailleuse Modèle 1950 (Löffler's roller-delayed design from the same wartime roots; possible battle rifle).

## Rifles to review later

- **Luger rifle:** a semi-auto rifle using the Luger's toggle action, in 7.92×57mm Mauser; only one survives.
- **Sterling rifle:** a 7.62×51mm NATO prototype built from Sterling SMG parts, using lever-delayed blowback and 30-round Bren mags; only two were made, one set up as an LMG.
- **Thompson rifle variants:** a .30-06 version meant to rival the BAR, a .30 Carbine version, and the "Calibre .30 Short Rifle".
