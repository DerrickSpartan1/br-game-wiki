# Guns to add later

Guns and offshoots found while researching, waiting for their turn. Offshoots name the category they'd likely go in.

- **Tokarev TT-33:** its cartridge was derived from the C96's 7.63mm, so they could share ammo.
- **Stechkin APS:** Soviet machine pistol with a holster-stock.
- **Welrod:** WWII British bolt-action pistol with a built-in suppressor.
- **Lancaster pistol:** four-barrel pistol.
- **Gyrojet:** fired small rockets instead of bullets.
- **Nagant M1895:** classic revolver.
- **Webley:** classic British revolver.
- **Colt Single Action Army:** classic revolver.
- **Janz-Schuknecht Pistole:** Janz's 9mm semi-auto.
- **FN Five-seven:** the P90's companion pistol, also in 5.7×28mm.
- **Ruger-57:** 5.7×28mm pistol, offered since 2019.
- **Kel-Tec P50:** 5.7×28mm pistol (2021) that takes the P90's 50-round mags.
- **PSA Rock 5.7:** Palmetto State Armory's 5.7×28mm pistol (2022).
- **S&W M&P 5.7:** Smith & Wesson's 5.7×28mm pistol (2023).
- **AR-57:** AR-platform 5.7×28mm rifle fed from 50-round P90 mags.
- **CMMG Banshee (5.7):** AR-style guns offered in 5.7×28mm.
- **ST Kinetics CPW:** multi-caliber SMG/PDW that can be set up for 5.7×28mm by swapping the barrel and mag.
- **Excel Arms 5.7 guns:** four 5.7×28mm firearms; models to research.
- **MasterPiece Arms 5.7 guns:** three 5.7×28mm firearms; models to research.
- **HK UCP:** the MP7's cancelled companion pistol in 4.6×30mm; prototype only.
- **PSA X5.7 PDW:** MP7-style concept gun in 5.7×28mm shown in 2024.
- **KRISS KARD:** semi-auto pistol announced in 2010 using a smaller Super V system, in 9×19 and .45 ACP; status to research.
- **PP-19-01 Vityaz:** the Bizon's 9×19 successor with box mags; Russia's standard SMG since 2005.
- **PPK-20:** subcompact modernization of the Vityaz.
- **StG 45(M):** late-war roller-delayed prototype in 7.92×33mm Kurz; its action led to HK's G3 and MP5.
- **Volkssturmgewehr 1-5:** last-ditch gas-delayed rifle in 7.92×33mm Kurz.
- **MKb 42(W):** Walther's competing design to the StG 44's predecessor.
- **Ingram SAM rifles:** M1 carbine derivatives in 5.56×45mm (M16 mags), 7.62×39mm (AK mags), and 7.62×51mm (FAL mags).
- **Hezi SM-1:** Israeli bullpup conversion of the M1 carbine.
- **vz. 58 offshoots:** EZ-B bullpup prototype, KLEČ light machine gun (longer barrel, like the RPK), and vz. 58/97 marksman rifle (possible DMR).
- **AR-18 offshoots:** AR-18 LSW prototype (quick-change barrel, later converted to 7.62×39mm; possible LMG), Singapore's SAR-80, SAR-87, and SR-88, and the Australian Bushmaster M17S bullpup. The Howa Type 89 is already on the possible-additions list.
- **Stoner 63 LMG configurations (LMG category):** belt-fed LMG (open bolt, 75-, 100-, or 150-round belt containers, quick-change barrel), tripod MMG, vehicle fixed machine gun, and the SEALs' Commando / Mk 23 Mod 0 (100-round box, no quick-change barrel).
- **Stoner 63 descendants:** Knight's Armament Stoner LMG (based on the later Stoner 86) and the semi-auto Robinson Armament M96 Expeditionary Rifle.
- **M16 / M4 offshoots:** Colt 9mm SMG (SMG category), Mk 12 SPR and M16A1 Special High and Low Profile (DMR category), Philippine Marine Scout Sniper Rifle, Taiwan's T65, T86, and T91, and Iran's KH-2002 bullpup.
- **HK33 offshoots:** HK33SG/1 marksman rifle (DMR category), and the HK13 light machine gun (box or 100-round drum, quick-change barrel), belt-fed HK23, and Myanmar MA-12 (LMG category).
- **Galil offshoots:** Galatz / Galil Sniper (semi-auto 7.62×51mm with a heavy match barrel and two-stage trigger), its SR-99 update and Vietnamese SBT-7.62VN clone (sniper or DMR category), and the 5.56mm Marksman Assault Rifle Mk 1 (DMR category).
- **AK-74 offshoots:** RPK-74 and RPK-16 (LMG category), and the AM-17 compact (could go with the AK-12).
- **AUG offshoots:** AUG HBAR and HBAR-T (24.4 in heavy barrel, bipod, fires from an open bolt; LMG category), and the AICW (experimental F88 with a Metal Storm 30mm barrel on top; possible special or crate weapon).
- **FAMAS offshoots:** FA-MAS Type 62 (7.62×51mm prototype from the 1950s–60s; battle rifle category) and the HS Produkt VHS (Croatian bullpup).
- **SA80 offshoots:** L86 LSW (25.4 in heavy barrel and bipod; LMG or DMR category), EM-2 (1950s British .280 bullpup, briefly adopted as the Rifle No. 9), and the EIW97 (1990s SA80 testbed firing electrically primed ammo, with programmable bursts at 300–400 RPM).
- **AS Val offshoots:** VSS Vintorez (suppressed 9×39mm sniper rifle, usually semi-auto with 10-round mags but capable of full auto), VSSM (modernized), and KO VSS (civilian semi-auto); DMR or sniper category.
- **SG 550 offshoots:** SG 550-1 Sniper (25.6 in heavy barrel; DMR or sniper category), SG 750 (6.5mm Creedmoor) and SG 751 SAPR (7.62×51mm, the Swiss Army's DMR; battle rifle or DMR category), the SG 540/542/543 predecessors, and the SG 510 / Stgw 57 (7.5×55mm Swiss battle rifle).
- **QBZ-95 offshoots:** QJB-95, QJB-95-1, and QJB-97 squad machine guns (bipod, heavier barrel, 75- or 80-round drums; LMG category), the K12 PUMA (12-gauge bullpup shotgun styled after the QBZ-95; shotgun category), and the QBZ-191 (China's newer conventional-layout rifle; possible AR addition).
- **G36 offshoots:** MG36 (heavy barrel squad machine gun with the C-Mag; LMG category), SL8 (semi-auto sporting rifle with a thumbhole stock, 20.1 in heavy barrel, and 10-round mags; possible DMR), the HK XM8 (US prototype rifle based on the G36), and the Mexican FX-05 Xiuhcoatl.
- **Tavor offshoots:** Tavor 7 (7.62×51mm bullpup with 17 in or 20 in barrel; battle rifle category).

## Rifles to review later

- **Luger rifle:** a semi-auto rifle using the Luger's toggle action, in 7.92×57mm Mauser; only one survives.
- **Sterling rifle:** a 7.62×51mm NATO prototype built from Sterling SMG parts, using lever-delayed blowback and 30-round Bren mags; only two were made, one set up as an LMG.
- **Thompson rifle variants:** a .30-06 version meant to rival the BAR, a .30 Carbine version, and the "Calibre .30 Short Rifle".
