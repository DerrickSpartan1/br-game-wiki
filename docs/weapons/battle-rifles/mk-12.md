# Mk 12 SPR

| Spec | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Category | DMR (light marksman rifle) |
| Cartridge | 5.56×45mm (all versions) |
| History | USA; a heavily modified [M16](../assault-rifles/m16-m4.md); Mark Westrom, president of ArmaLite, proposed the concept in 2000 while working at Rock Island Arsenal, and the Naval Surface Warfare Center Crane Division developed it for special operations troops who wanted more range than the M4 carbine in a rifle shorter than the [SR-25](sr-25.md); it grew out of the SOPMOD Block I program and the SEALs' Recon Rifle, and lessons from Somalia shaped its optics, ammo, trigger, and rails; it began as an add-on upper, the "Special Purpose Receiver," and became the "Special Purpose Rifle" when fielded as a whole rifle, which the Navy type-classified as the Mk 12; first tested in October 2000 and first issued in 2002; Colt made most of the upper receivers and Diemaco the rest; used by Army Special Forces, the Rangers, Navy SEALs, and the Marines (late in the Iraq War and widely in Afghanistan); phased out in 2017 |
| Magazine (rounds) | 20- or 30-round STANAG box, the same mags as the M16 |
| Fire mode / rate | Semi-auto, with auto kept for emergencies on the Mk 12: its lower receivers were mostly M16A1 or M4A1 types, kept for their full-auto capability and fitted with a Knight's Armament two-stage match trigger; auto rate not given in the sources; most other versions are semi-auto only; direct impingement gas with a rotating bolt, closed bolt, so no open-bolt delay |
| Damage | TBD |
| Effective range | Real-world 700 m (Mk 12), 550–600 m (SAM-R and SDM-R), 600–800 m (MSSR); in-game TBD |

## Real-world notes

About 4.5 kg loaded with its heavy barrel and scope; 952.5 mm long; 18 in (457 mm) match-grade, free-floating stainless heavy barrel with a 1:7 twist and a threaded muzzle (16 in on the Mod H); sub-MOA accuracy; built around the Mk 262 round, a 77-grain open-tip match load from Black Hills Ammunition made to replace M855 for accuracy and hitting power, at about 838 m/s; takes any M16-type stock; SEAL teams were reportedly disappointed with it and pushed Crane to keep developing their Recon Rifle (flavor only)

## In-game traits

One roster entry that rolls one of 7 versions (see [Versions](#versions) below). It holds the M16-based marksman rifles, the way the [M14](m14.md) page holds the M21 and Mk 14. It shares 5.56×45mm with most assault rifles. The military versions spawn with a scope, since each was issued as a scoped rifle. Versions keep their real fire modes (see [Rules](../../rules.md#versions)): the Mk 12, Mod H, and M16A1 Special keep auto, and the rest roll semi-auto only.

## Attachments

DMR/sniper family attachments; native Picatinny rail on most versions, a carry-handle scope mount on the M16A1 Special High Profile and early MSSRs. The NFWS has no muzzle or suppressor slot, since its suppressor is built in. Unique: the OPS Inc. 12th Model suppressor (and its Allen Engineering AEM5 successor), the Harris swivel bipod, the Leupold TS-30A2 scope, the Nightforce 2.5–10×42 NXS scope, the KAC 600 m flip-up rear sight, the SDM-R's 4×32 ACOG, and the MSSR's AAC SR-5 suppressor. Mag tiers (all real STANAG mags, shared with the M16): 20 standard; 30-round as makeshift extended and quick; 40-round as extended quick; 60-round as extended

## Versions

7 versions after merging. US and Philippine M16-based marksman rifles differ only in small details, so they merge by barrel length and role. The West Coast SAM-R was an M16A4 with a rail and ACOG, so it stays with the M16. The [HK416](../assault-rifles/hk416.md)-based M38 SDMR stays on the [guns to add later](../../planning/add-later.md) list, and the M110A1 SDMR is H&K's G28, which goes with the HK417 in the [queue](../../planning/queue.md). PRI's .308 Winchester Mk 12 (shown at SHOT Show 2024) is left out, since 7.62mm ARs belong with the [AR-10](ar-10.md).
